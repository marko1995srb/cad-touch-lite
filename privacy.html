CAD Touch Lite

Privacy policy

Last updated: DATUM_OBJAVE · Publisher: Marko Nedic software

This policy covers all versions of the app: CAD Touch Lite for Windows from the Microsoft Store, the Android app from Google Play, and CAD Touch for iPhone and iPad from the App Store (the same app also runs on Mac computers with Apple silicon). Where the versions differ, the paragraph starts with "Windows", "Android" or "iPhone and iPad".

Summary

Your drawings are opened and processed only on your own device. CAD Touch Lite for Windows and the iPhone and iPad app do not collect, store or share any personal data. The Android app (from version 1.9) can send basic usage statistics, never any drawing content (see "Android usage statistics"), and from version 1.11 it downloads promotion settings (see "Promotion settings"). Version 2.0 of the Android app contains Google's advertising software, which is switched off in this version and sends nothing; what would change if ads are switched on is described under "Ads on Android (version 2.0 and newer)".

Apart from the Android statistics and the download of promotion settings, the app itself sends nothing unless you choose to share something, except what the app stores need for purchases: on Android, Google Play handles the subscription, and in versions that offer it, Google's billing component inside the app also sends technical diagnostic data to Google; on iPhone and iPad, the App Store handles the subscription (see "Purchases and subscriptions"). None of this reaches us; when something is bought, we only receive the stores' sales reports, described below.

Your drawings

Windows: DWG and DXF files you open are read locally. When a DWG file is opened for the first time, a converted DXF copy is saved in your Documents folder (Documents\CAD Touch Lite) so it can be reopened quickly. You can delete these copies at any time from the app or from File Explorer. No drawing, file name or file content is ever uploaded anywhere.

Android: You open a drawing (DWG or DXF, and from version 1.11 also a PDF plan or a picture of a plan in JPG or PNG) with Android's file picker, or from another app with "Open with" or "Share"; the app gets access only to the files you choose. From version 1.11 you can also use "Add folder": you choose one folder in Android's folder picker, the app remembers the access that Android gives it to that folder (this is stored only on your device) and adds the drawings, PDFs and pictures it finds in that folder and its subfolders, also new ones later. When you remove the folder in Settings › Drawing folders, the app gives that access back. So that you can reopen it and keep notes on it, the app normally saves a copy of the file in its own folder on your device (the location is shown in the app under About) and keeps a prepared copy in its cache so that it opens again quickly. The original file is only read, never changed. Drawings are read entirely on your device: no drawing, file name or file content is uploaded anywhere. If you open a file from a cloud drive, that drive's app downloads it; CAD Touch Lite itself does not connect to the internet to open drawings.

iPhone and iPad: You open a drawing with the Files picker, or from another app with "Open in" or "Share"; the app gets access only to the file you choose. The app saves a copy of the file in its own folder, which you can see in the Files app under On My iPhone (or On My iPad) › CAD Touch › Drawings, and keeps a prepared copy in its cache so that it opens again quickly. The original file is only read, never changed. Drawings are read entirely on your device: no drawing, file name or file content is uploaded anywhere. If you open a file from iCloud Drive or another cloud service, that service downloads it; CAD Touch itself does not connect to the internet to open drawings.

Notes and pictures

Windows: Notes you pin to a drawing, and the pictures you attach to them, are stored only on your device, in a folder beside the converted drawing (Documents\CAD Touch Lite). "Take photo" opens the Windows Camera app; the app itself does not use the camera. A note leaves your device only when you choose Share, Print or Save, and then it goes only where you send it. Removing a drawing from the app moves its notes to the Recycle Bin.

Android: Notes and their pictures are stored only on your device, in the app's folder beside the drawing copy. "Take photo" opens your device's camera app, which hands the photo to CAD Touch Lite; the app has no camera permission and cannot use the camera by itself (some camera apps also keep their own copy in your gallery). "Add picture" opens Android's photo picker, and the app receives only the pictures you select. The app keeps its own copy of each picture as a JPEG without location (GPS) or other metadata. If a photo cannot be added to a note, the app keeps it in your gallery folder Pictures/CAD Touch (on Android 8 and 9, in the app's folder) and tells you where it is. A note leaves your device only when you choose Share, Print, Save picture, Save as PDF or Copy text, and then it goes only where you send it. Drawings you remove from the app, and pictures you delete, go to the app's own trash and are deleted permanently after 30 days.

iPhone and iPad: Notes and their pictures are stored only on your device, in the app's Drawings folder beside the drawing copy. "Take photo" uses the camera inside the app, so the first time iOS asks whether CAD Touch may use the camera; the app uses it only while you take a photo for a note, and you can turn this off at any time in Settings › CAD Touch. "Add picture" opens Apple's photo picker, which needs no permission: the app receives only the pictures you select and cannot see the rest of your photo library. The app keeps its own copy of each picture as a JPEG without location (GPS) or other metadata, and it does not save photos to your photo library. A note leaves your device only when you choose Share, Print, Save picture, Save as PDF or Copy text, and then it goes only where you send it. Drawings you remove from the app go to the app's own trash and are deleted permanently after 30 days.

Measurements

Android, iPhone and iPad: Measurements you keep on a drawing (distances, perimeters, areas, diameters and point coordinates, in versions that save them) are stored only on your device, in the app's folder beside the drawing copy, together with that drawing's notes. They are saved as a small text file with the measured points in the drawing's own coordinates; the drawing file itself is never changed. Measurements that are not saved (without Pro the app keeps only one, and never saves it) stay on the screen only while the drawing is open and are never written to storage. Measurements are not uploaded anywhere. You can tap a measurement and delete it, or delete all measurements of a drawing; removing a drawing from the app moves its saved measurements to the app's trash together with its notes, where they are deleted permanently after 30 days.

Data we collect

Windows: None. The app contains no analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting services, and it does not require an account with us.

Android: No ads at present and no account with us (version 2.0 can show ads to free users if they are switched on later, see "Ads on Android (version 2.0 and newer)"). From version 1.9 the app can send basic usage statistics, which you can turn off (see "Android usage statistics" below); earlier versions send none. From version 1.11 the app also downloads promotion settings from Google Firebase Remote Config, whatever your statistics choice (see "Promotion settings" below). Versions that offer the Pro subscription contain the Google Play Billing Library, a component made by Google, which sends technical diagnostic data about the billing component to Google (see "Purchases and subscriptions"). Because Google counts data that a component inside an app sends as data collected by that app, the app's Data safety section on Google Play lists it as diagnostics. We do not receive it. Separately, if you have allowed your device to share usage and diagnostics data with Google, Android may report crashes and freezes of your apps, including this one, to Google, and Google shows developers these reports in Google Play Console. That is a setting of your device, not part of the app.

iPhone and iPad: None. The app contains no analytics, tracking, advertising or crash-reporting services and no third-party code that sends data; the app's own code makes no internet connections. Purchases go through Apple's StoreKit, which is part of iOS (see "Purchases and subscriptions"). The app's App Privacy section on the App Store therefore says "Data Not Collected". If you have chosen to share analytics with app developers in Settings › Privacy & Security › Analytics & Improvements, Apple may share crash reports and usage statistics with us in anonymous form; that is a setting of your device, not part of the app.

Android test versions: If you take part in a test of the Android app, we know the e-mail address of the Google account that you gave us for the test list or that joined our test group, and we use it only to run the test. Google Play tells testers that it collects certain data about their use of a test version and shares it with the developer; in Google Play Console we see statistics such as installs and crashes, and the feedback you choose to send us.

Purchases and subscriptions

Windows: Subscriptions, the free trial and the one-time purchase, when they become available, are sold and managed by the Microsoft Store. Payment information is handled by Microsoft under the Microsoft Privacy Statement. The app only asks the Microsoft Store whether you own one of them; we do not receive your payment details.

Android: Measuring and notes are the paid Pro features. When the subscription is available in the version you have (test versions may have everything unlocked), it is sold and managed by Google Play, and payment information is handled by Google under the Google Privacy Policy. The app asks Google Play whether your Google account has an active subscription and stores the answer on your device (whether Pro is active and when this was last checked), so that measuring and notes keep working for up to 14 days without a connection to Google Play. We do not receive your name, e-mail address or payment details. You can cancel the subscription at any time in the Google Play app under Payments & subscriptions → Subscriptions.

Android: Google Play gives every developer reports about the sales of their apps. For each order they contain the order number, date, product, price and taxes, the device model and the buyer's country, and for some orders the region, city and postal code. They contain no names or e-mail addresses. We use these reports only for accounting and taxes and keep them as long as tax and accounting rules require. If you contact us about a purchase and give us its order number or the e-mail address of your Google account, we can look up that order in Google Play Console, for example to refund it.

iPhone and iPad: Measuring and notes are the paid Pro features. The subscription is sold and managed by Apple through the App Store, with your Apple Account; payment information is handled by Apple under the Apple Privacy Policy. The app asks the App Store whether your Apple Account has an active subscription and stores the answer on your device (whether Pro is active and when this was last checked), so that measuring and notes keep working for up to 35 days without a connection to the App Store. We do not receive your name, e-mail address or payment details. Apple gives developers sales reports with totals per product, country and date, without names or contact details. Refunds are requested from and decided by Apple. You can cancel the subscription at any time in Settings › your name › Subscriptions.

Android: Versions that offer the subscription contain the Google Play Billing Library, Google's component for purchases in apps. It sends technical diagnostic data about its own work to Google over an encrypted (HTTPS) connection. Google does not publish exactly what this data contains. The app never passes your drawings, notes, measurements or pictures to it. Google processes this data under the Google Privacy Policy; we do not receive it and cannot see or delete it.

Permissions

Android: Versions without the subscription ask for no permissions at all. Versions with the subscription have these permissions, all of which Google's billing component requires: "Google Play billing service", "full network access" and "view network connections" (used by Google's billing component, from version 1.9 by Google Analytics for Firebase to send the usage statistics described below, and from version 1.11 by Firebase Remote Config to download promotion settings; the app's own code makes no other internet connections), plus one internal permission that only this app itself can use. From version 1.9 Firebase also adds "prevent phone from sleeping" (WAKE_LOCK, used briefly while statistics are sent) and the Google Play install referrer (which tells the app which store link or ad led to the installation). Version 2.0 adds the permission "advertising ID" (com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID), which lets Google's ads software read the Android advertising ID if ads are switched on later; it is not used while ads are off (see "Ads on Android (version 2.0 and newer)"). The app has no permission for the camera, microphone, location, contacts or your files: photos come from your camera app or the photo picker, and drawings from the file picker, from a folder you chose with "Add folder" (Android then gives access only to that folder, not to all your files) or from the app you open them from.

iPhone and iPad: The only permission the app asks for is the camera, and only when you tap "Take photo". It does not ask for access to your photo library, location, contacts, microphone or other apps' files.

Settings stored on your device

Windows: The app stores a few local settings (chosen language, measurement units per drawing, list of recently opened drawings) and a cache that lets drawings you have already opened reopen faster. All of it is kept in your user profile and never leaves your device.

Android: The app stores its settings (language, display unit, units per drawing, light or dark background), the list of drawings in its folder, from version 1.11 your favourite and recently opened drawings, the folders you added with "Add folder", how often you tapped a locked Pro feature, how often each promotion was shown, and whether you chose "Notify me" for the AI plan (only a mark on your device: the app asks for no e-mail address and sends no notification), the place you were looking at while another app is in front, a few technical notes (for example how long drawings took to load, to estimate the next loading time) and, in versions with the subscription, whether Pro is active. All of it stays in the app's storage on your device. The app does not use Android's cloud backup. If you move to a new phone with Android's device-to-device transfer (Android 12 and newer), your drawings, notes, measurements, pictures and settings can be copied to the new phone as part of that transfer.

iPhone and iPad: The app stores its settings (display unit, units per drawing, light or dark background), the list of drawings in its folder, the place you were looking at if iOS closes the app in the background, a few technical notes (for example how long drawings took to load) and whether Pro is active. The language is the one you choose for the app in iOS Settings or in the app. All of it stays in the app's storage on your device. If you back up your device to iCloud or to a computer, iOS includes the app's drawings, notes, measurements, pictures and settings in that backup, as it does for other apps; caches are not backed up.

Ratings

Windows: The app may occasionally ask whether you would like to rate it. The rating window belongs to the Microsoft Store; what you write there is sent to Microsoft, not to us.

Android: The Android app may ask whether you would like to rate it, at most once in 30 days and only after a first report, a third note or the fifteenth time a drawing is opened, using Google Play's own rating window; you can also use "Rate on Google Play" in Settings at any time; Google decides whether that window appears. In the released app, what you write there goes to Google, not to us, and the app does not learn whether or how you rated it. In a test version of the app, Google shares your rating and comment privately with us as test feedback. If you write a review on Google Play, Google publishes it under your Google profile name, and we can read it and reply there.

iPhone and iPad: The app may ask once whether you would like to rate it, after you have made a few notes, using Apple's own rating window; Apple decides whether that window appears, and the app does not learn whether or how you rated it. The "Rate on the App Store" button in About opens the App Store. Ratings and reviews you write there go to Apple, which publishes reviews under your App Store nickname.

Android usage statistics (version 1.9 and newer)

Your choice. For users in the European Union and European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the Android app asks the first time you start it whether you allow usage statistics. Until you answer, and if you decline, nothing is sent. Elsewhere, usage statistics are on by default. Everyone can turn them off at any time in the app under Settings; turning it off stops sending and tells the service to stop collecting. The app works the same either way.

What is sent. Events such as: first opening of the app, introduction finished, drawing opened (whether it was the built-in demo drawing, your own drawing, a PDF or a picture, without saying which file), the first time you open a drawing of your own, a drawing that could not be opened (only the kind of error, the file type and a rough size class, never the file name), note saved (with a photo or without), report sent, a locked Pro feature tapped, Pro offer shown or closed, plan tapped, trial started, purchase, a purchase that did not go through and the reason given by Google Play (for example cancelled or no connection), a pending purchase, trial ended and subscription cancelled. From version 1.11 also: the file picker or folder picker opened, a folder added (only a rough count of the files found in it, such as "a few" or "many"), the guide "How to open a DWG" opened (and from which place in the app), whether a report was made for all contractors, one or several (never their names), a promotion shown, tapped or closed (only the campaign's identifier and whether it was a window, a bar or a badge), the "AI plan" row or its "Notify me" button tapped, and a subscription renewed. From version 1.12 also: a scale set for measuring on a PDF plan or a picture (only whether it was set with a known length or with a ratio such as 1:100, and the unit, never the value or the drawing). Trial started, purchase and subscription renewed also carry the kind of offer (trial, promotion or regular price), Google Play's identifier of the offer, the price and the currency. Together with the statistics, the app also sends a rough count of how often you tapped a locked Pro feature (none, one, a few or many), so that we can offer a discount to people who are interested in Pro. Google Analytics for Firebase also records a few events by itself: session start, screen view, time in the app (engagement), app update and app removal. The service also adds technical data that Google Analytics for Firebase collects by itself: the app version, Android version, device model, language, approximate region derived from the network address (the address itself is not stored), and an identifier of this app installation. This identifier is not your name and not an advertising ID, but it is a pseudonymous identifier, so we treat these statistics as personal data and do not call them anonymous.

What is never sent. Drawing content, drawing, file or folder names, the text of notes, names of contractors, photos, coordinates, layer names, measurements, your e-mail address or your Google account. The statistics never use the Android advertising ID and are never used for personalised ads or remarketing. CAD Touch itself never receives the advertising ID; see "Ads on Android (version 2.0 and newer)" for the advertising ID that Google's ads software may read once ads are switched on.

Measuring our ads. Outside the EU/EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, the events "trial started" and "purchase" are also shared with Google Ads as conversions, only so that we can see which of our ad campaigns lead to a trial or purchase. In the EU/EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland this is done only if you choose "Allow and continue" in the consent window of version 1.10 or newer; consent given in version 1.9 covers only the statistics, not ad measurement. No advertising storage or ad personalisation is ever enabled.

Purpose and legal basis. We use the statistics to see how many people open the app and where they stop (for example before the first drawing), so that we can improve it. Where you are asked (EU/EEA, United Kingdom, Switzerland), the legal basis is your consent (GDPR Art. 6(1)(a) and the equivalent UK and Swiss rules), which you can withdraw at any time as described above; withdrawing does not affect what was sent before. Elsewhere, we rely on our legitimate interest in improving the app (or the equivalent basis under the rules of your country), and you can object at any time by turning the statistics off.

Who processes it. Google (Google Ireland Limited for users in the EEA and the United Kingdom, Google LLC elsewhere) processes the data on our behalf as a processor, through Firebase and Google Analytics, under Google's terms for data processing. Data may be processed in the United States and other countries where Google operates. See Google Privacy Policy and Privacy and security in Firebase.

How long. Event-level data is kept in Google Analytics for 2 months (Google's default and shortest setting; the maximum is 14 months). After that only aggregated reports without the installation identifier remain. Because the statistics are tied to an installation identifier, we cannot look up a person by name; to have the data of your installation deleted, write to us; clearing the app's storage or uninstalling the app removes the installation identifier from the device, so later events can no longer be linked to the earlier ones.

Promotion settings (version 1.11 and newer). The Android app downloads promotion settings from Google Firebase Remote Config, for example the dates and text of a discount campaign. This does not depend on the statistics choice, because the app needs these settings to know whether to show a promotion at all. For this, Firebase uses a Firebase installation identifier and basic technical data such as the app version, language and country, so that the right settings are chosen. No name, e-mail address, files or drawing content are sent, and no statistics event is sent because of this download. Promotions are never shown to Pro subscribers. We rely on our legitimate interest in showing current offers in the app (GDPR Art. 6(1)(f)). Google processes this data on our behalf, as described under "Who processes it".

Updates while the app is open (version 2.0 and newer). While the app is on screen, it keeps one open internet connection to Firebase Remote Config, so that a changed promotion setting can reach it within seconds. The connection is closed or not used when the app is not on screen, and the app also fetches the settings when it comes back on screen (at most once an hour). Like the download itself, this does not depend on the statistics choice, uses the Firebase installation identifier, and sends no statistics event. The switches for ads (see below) arrive in the same way; no additional data is sent for them.

Windows, iPhone and iPad: no usage statistics and no tracking.

Ads on Android (version 2.0 and newer)

Now: ads are switched off. The Android app contains Google's Mobile Ads SDK and Google's User Messaging Platform (consent) SDK. While ads are switched off, as at the release of version 2.0, both stay inactive: the app starts no ads software, asks for no ad consent, makes no ad requests and makes no contact with Google's advertising or consent servers. The permission com.google.android.gms.permission.AD_ID lets Google's ads software read the Android advertising ID if ads are switched on later; it is never used while ads are off. Everything below applies only from the day ads are switched on, which we do with a setting we send through Firebase Remote Config; this page will then still describe it, and its date shows when it was last changed.

If ads are switched on: who sees them. Ads served by Google AdMob may be shown only to users without Pro and not in the free trial, and only after Google Play has told the app that. They may appear as a banner at the bottom of the home screen (never over a drawing), as a full-screen ad on the home screen after you close a drawing (not on every close and not more than a few times a day), and as an ad you can choose to watch in exchange for a small reward in the Pro-feature window (for example one more note on that drawing or one more measurement or export; the reward is given only after the ad was watched). Pro subscribers and users in the free trial never see ads, never get the ad consent form, and buying Pro removes the banner at once. The state of the rewards (daily counts and single-use rewards) stays on your device and is sent nowhere.

What Google's ads software collects. When ads are shown, Google's Mobile Ads SDK collects and shares with Google your IP address (from which Google may estimate an approximate location), your interactions with the app and the ads (such as app launches, taps and video views), diagnostic data, and the device identifiers Android advertising ID and app set ID, for advertising, analytics and fraud prevention. The data is encrypted in transit. Google processes it under its own policies; see Google's disclosure about data collected by the Mobile Ads SDK and How Google uses information from sites or apps that use our services. CAD Touch itself never receives the advertising ID.

Your choice. In the European Economic Area, the United Kingdom and Switzerland, Google's consent form (Google's certified consent platform) asks for your choice before ads are requested. It appears at a calm moment on the home screen, never together with our statistics dialog, and is shown in your phone's language. You can change your choice at any time in Settings under "Ad privacy choices"; this row exists only while ads are on for you. Your choice in our own statistics dialog is separate from Google's form: our statistics never use Google's consent string, the app keeps advertising storage and ad personalisation denied in Firebase, and the statistics are never used for personalised ads.

Statistics about ads. If you allowed usage statistics (outside the EU/EEA, the United Kingdom and Switzerland they are on by default and can be switched off in Settings), the app records only whether an ad was shown or failed to load (its format and a reason code) and whether a reward was offered, watched or granted (only its type). Google's ads software also records its own ad events in Google Analytics while ads are on: ad_impression, ad_query, ad_exposure, adunit_exposure, ad_reward and, if an ad is tapped, ad_click. These events exist only while ads are on and only if statistics are on for you.

Keeping and deleting your data

Android: We run no server and keep no data about you, apart from Google Play's sales reports and, if you take part in a test, your test e-mail address (both described above); ask us and we remove you from a test list. Everything the app stores is on your device and stays there until you delete it:

  • In the app: remove a drawing from the list to delete its copy together with its notes, measurements and pictures. Removed items stay in the app's trash for 30 days and are then deleted permanently. To delete a single measurement, tap it and choose "Delete measurement"; you can undo this for a few seconds, after that it is gone.
  • Everything at once: in Android Settings → Apps → CAD Touch Lite → Storage, choose "Clear storage" (on some devices "Clear data"). This immediately deletes all drawing copies, notes, measurements, pictures, settings and caches of the app.
  • Uninstalling the app deletes all of it as well, unless you choose to keep the app's data when Android asks (Android 10 and newer).
  • Pictures kept in your gallery folder Pictures/CAD Touch, and files you saved or shared elsewhere, are not part of the app's storage; delete them in your gallery or file manager.
  • Usage statistics: switch them off in the app under Settings; retention is described under "Android usage statistics".
  • Data held by Google (your subscription, your payments and the billing component's diagnostic data) is kept and deleted under the Google Privacy Policy; you can manage your Google data in your Google Account.

iPhone and iPad: We keep no data about you, apart from Apple's sales reports (described above). Everything the app stores is on your device:

  • In the app: remove a drawing from the list to delete its copy together with its notes, measurements and pictures. Removed items stay in the app's trash for 30 days and are then deleted permanently. To delete a single measurement, tap it and choose "Delete measurement".
  • In the Files app you can also see and delete the drawings and their notes under On My iPhone (or On My iPad) › CAD Touch › Drawings.
  • Everything at once: delete the app (touch and hold its icon, then Remove App › Delete App). This deletes all drawing copies, notes, measurements, pictures, settings and caches of the app. Copies inside an older iCloud or computer backup are deleted when that backup is replaced or deleted.
  • Data held by Apple (your subscription and your payments) is kept and deleted under the Apple Privacy Policy; you can manage it at privacy.apple.com.

Security

Android: The app keeps your drawings, notes, measurements and pictures in its own folder on your device. On Android 11 and newer, other apps normally cannot open that folder; on older versions, apps that you have allowed to access your device's storage can read it. On every version, a computer connected to the device by USB can see it. Settings and the subscription status are kept in the app's private storage. We run no server that receives your data.

iPhone and iPad: The app's files are kept in its own container, which other apps cannot open; you can see the Drawings folder in the Files app. Settings and the subscription status are kept in a part of the app's storage that the Files app does not show. iOS encrypts the app's data with your device passcode (Data Protection).
